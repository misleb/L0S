# The programs

Each one is the usual three files, `NAME.l0`, `NAME_test.l0` and
`main.l0`, in a directory whose place says where the program goes in the
initramfs, so that the Makefile finds it there:

| Directory | What | In the initramfs |
|---|---|---|
| `user/bin/NAME` | a command: `sh`, `cat`, `disk`, `draw`, `fonts`, `hello`, `http`, `init`, `install`, `keys`, `lsusb`, `nap`, `net`, `ping`, `play`, `random`, `spin`, `tftp`, `windows` | `/bin/NAME` |
| `user/sbin/NAME` | a driver or a service: `boot`, `uart`, `rtc`, `eeprom`, `virtio-gpu`, `hvs`, `hdmi-audio`, `virtio-input`, `virtio-blk`, `virtio-net`, `gem`, `virtio-rng`, `pcie`, `xhci`, `usb-hid`, `sdhci`, `fs`, `inet`, `font`, `wm`, `echo` | `/sbin/NAME` |
| `user/apps/NAME` | an app, with its bundle's source `APP.app/` beside it: `vt`, `procmon`, `widgets`, `disks`, `files` (see [Apps](boot.md#apps)) | `/Applications/APP.app/NAME` |

A program may have more files of its own, each with its tests beside it,
as `sh` has `files.l0`, `wm` `launcher.l0`, `files` `fsview.l0`, `inet` `packet.l0`, `segment.l0`, `tcp.l0`, `tcpstack.l0`, `dhcp.l0` and `lease.l0`, `pcie` `cfg.l0`, `sdhci` `sd.l0`, `usb-hid` `hid.l0`, `rtc` `firmware.l0`, `eeprom` `image.l0`, `hvs` `regs.l0`, `plane.l0`, `pieces.l0`, `corners.l0`, `iommu.l0` and `fw.l0`, `hdmi-audio` `mai.l0`, `iec958.l0` and `dma40.l0`, `gem` `regs.l0` and `phy.l0`, and `xhci` `trb.l0`, `ring.l0`, `ctx.l0`, `port.l0`, `regs.l0`, `core.l0`, `bus.l0` and `dwc3.l0`:
every `.l0` in its directory is part of it.

| Program | What it does |
|---|---|
| `boot` | the first stage's end, pid 1 of a two-stage inittab (see [Booting from a volume](boot.md#booting-from-a-volume)): given `root=UUID`, waits for that volume, makes it `/` if it holds `/etc/system`, and says so or why not; given `root=tmpfs`, has `install` fill the RAM file system first; starts `/etc/system`'s programs by path, and exits with the first one's status |
| `sh` | the shell, on the terminal its argument names, `console` unless given (`sh vt1` is a window's): reads commands from it; runs programs by name or path, from `/` wherever it is or from a volume, on its terminal, and waits for them, with Ctrl-C for the foreground job; has busybox's commands on files, `ls`, `cat`, `write`, `append`, `cp`, `mv`, `rm`, `mkdir`, `df`, `format`, `unmount` and `relabel`, on the initramfs and the volumes alike; also `ps`, `kill`, `sleep`, `echo`, `exit`, `reboot` and `dmesg`, which prints the kernel's log, a line for each message with its time since boot. Shows `/etc/motd` at start. Exits with status 129 if its terminal's process exits |
| `install` | `install VOLUME`: copies every directory and file of `/` but `/Volumes` and `/boot`, the Pi 5's boot files, onto the volume, a label or `/Volumes/LABEL`, marking each program's copy a program, and says the volume's UUID, for `root=`; refused for a volume not mounted, and for `/`'s own. `install --boot VOLUME [FROM]` makes a FAT32 volume a Raspberry Pi 5's boot partition instead: FROM's files, `/boot` unless given, copied onto it, the Pi 5's device trees among them, and `input.bin` written, an initramfs of `/` but `/Volumes`, a file at a time; it says so if the card is left with no device tree, as from a FROM without one. With `--root ROOT`, anywhere after VOLUME, the card's `cmdline.txt` says `root=` and ROOT's UUID, its other arguments kept and any `root=` replaced, so that `boot` makes ROOT `/`; refused, before anything is written, for a ROOT not mounted, with no UUID, or VOLUME itself (see [The Raspberry Pi 5](pi5.md#the-raspberry-pi-5)) |
| `uart` | the serial line's terminal, `console`: a driver for the PL011 serial port. L0 says nothing of the board, so it finds its port: of the places a PL011 is on L0's boards, QEMU's at 0x09000000 and the Raspberry Pi 5's debug UART at 0x107D001000, the first the kernel gives whose ID registers say PL011. It takes that port's receive interrupt, 33 or 153, edits lines, echoes them (unless given `noecho`), and hands a line to each process that asks. Ctrl-C drops unread input and sends a lone byte 3 to the port that asked to hear of it. What it is sent to write, it writes to the serial line |
| `vt` | Terminal, terminals in a window, as GNOME Console's, `vt [COLS ROWS]`, a window of 80×24 cells unless given. Its window draws its own decorations (`ui_open_csd`): the toolkit's header bar, the selected tab's name its title and wm's, with a new-tab button at its start and the close button at its end; with two tabs or more, the toolkit's tab bar under it, a click on a tab selecting it and its close button closing it; and under them the terminal, a drawing area `vt` paints itself. Each tab is a terminal of its own (`tabs.l0`), named the first of `vt1`, `vt2`, and so on that no one has: a relay, `vt tab PORT PID`, a `vt` of its own that `vt` starts for the tab, registers the name and passes on to `vt` all that is sent to it, since a process waits on one port and a name goes only with the process that registered it. Once the relay says its name, `vt` starts the tab's shell, `sh NAME`, and watches it: when it exits the tab closes, and with the last tab the window, `vt` exiting with that shell's status; the close button ends every tab's shell and relay, and the window. Each tab has the same line editing as `uart`'s, fed the characters of the keys `wm` sends while the window is in front and the tab selected. What a tab is sent to write, and the echo, it keeps as bytes (`cells.l0`): its screen, a row of cells each, a byte a cell, each row marked if a wrap, not a newline, ended it, and a ring of the last 10,000 rows scrolled off its top; output to a tab not shown is kept for when it is. The selected tab is drawn into the window's buffer, shared with `wm`, in the 8×16 font (at scale 2, cells of 16×32 in the face's Source Code Pro), in the toolkit's terminal colors, GNOME Console's white on `1e1e1e`: output at the bottom by copying the rows it scrolls and drawing those it wrote, each batch one `damage`. The wheel scrolls the view back through the ring, three rows a step, drawn from the cells with the toolkit's overlay scrollbar along its right while the view is back; a key typed brings the view back to the bottom, and output while it is back leaves the view on the rows it shows. A drag of the window's edge or maximizing it resizes it as any toolkit window, the terminal then as many cells as fit, every tab's lines wrapped again at the new width by their marks, the cursor's line kept in view. Its cursor is a line along the last row of a cell, which no glyph uses |
| `cat` | writes the files its arguments name, through the ordinary `open_in`, the initramfs's or a volume's; reports each it can't read, and exits 1 if any |
| `hello` | prints its pid and arguments, and answers a line: typed at its terminal, with `console_line` |
| `init` | starts the programs its arguments name, from `/` wherever it is (`NAME&` runs one in the background), waits for the rest, and reports each one's status |
| `echo` | a server: registers as `echo` and sends every request's body back |
| `ping` | a client: finds `echo` by name and checks that N requests come back |
| `spin` | loops forever without a call; other processes still run, thanks to the timer |
| `nap` | sleeps for N ms and checks that the time passed |
| `rtc` | a user-space driver for the PL031 real-time clock: it maps the device, sets an alarm, receives interrupt 34 as a message, and acks it. `rtc clock` instead serves `clock`, the time, to whoever asks, and at the start of every minute to each process listening, as `wm` does; it looks for both every quarter of a second, and leaves the interrupt to `rtc`. Its clock is a PL031 where the kernel gives one whose ID registers say so, or else the Raspberry Pi 5's RTC, asked of the firmware through the mailbox (`firmware.l0` over `mailbox.l0`, see [The Raspberry Pi 5](pi5.md#the-raspberry-pi-5)); with neither it says so and exits 0 |
| `eeprom` | the Raspberry Pi 5's bootloader configuration: with no argument, the one running, and with `version` the bootloader's version, asked of the firmware's command line through the mailbox; `show IMAGE` the configuration inside a `pieeprom.bin`; `update IMAGE CONFIG DIR` that image with the file CONFIG as its configuration, written to DIR as `pieeprom.upd` and `pieeprom.sig` for the bootloader to flash itself from (`image.l0`, see [The bootloader's configuration](pi5.md#the-bootloaders-configuration)). With no mailbox it says so and exits 1 |
| `virtio-gpu` | a user-space driver for QEMU's virtio-gpu, serving the display protocol as `display` (see [The desktop](desktop.md#the-desktop)). It finds the device among the 32 virtio-mmio transports and sets up its control queue and cursor queue in DMA pages. It makes the screen resource 1 on scanout 0, at the mode the device offers, and builds its backing from the layers' buffers, so the host composes the screen. Its `info` says the scale, 2 on a screen of 2560×1440 or more, or as the boot argument `scale=` says (see [A large screen](desktop.md#a-large-screen)). It keeps every row of the backing, so a `layers` request builds again only the rows a layer that moved, scrolled or changed covers, and writes the backing's runs straight into the queue's memory. A `damage` is a transfer and a flush of that rectangle. The pointer is the device's cursor, resource 2. Its first frame, as soon as the mode is set, is the wallpaper, under every layer it is given until one covers the screen (see [The display](display.md#the-display-protocol)); with none to read, it is black. With no virtio-gpu on the board it says so on stderr and exits 0. It polls the queues rather than taking interrupts. If the device offers virgl, as QEMU's `virtio-gpu-gl-device` does, it composes the screen on the host's GPU instead: a texture for each buffer and copies of them onto the screen (`gl.l0`, `compose.l0`, `virgl.l0`; see [The desktop](desktop.md#the-desktop)) |
| `hvs` | the Raspberry Pi 5's display driver, a first draft that has shown the desktop, brought up a stage at a time by its argument (`probe`, `list`, `iommu`; none serves; `mode` and `hdmi` beside them), serving the display protocol as `display` as `virtio-gpu` does (see [The display on the Raspberry Pi 5](display.md#the-display-on-the-raspberry-pi-5)). It reads the monitor's EDID from the firmware (`edid.l0`), sets HDMI0 to the monitor's preferred mode itself, PHY, timings, pixel valve and, past 340 MHz, scrambling over DDC (`hdmi.l0`, `modeset.l0`; the boot argument `hdmi=` chooses otherwise), and takes HDMI0's channel of the BCM2712's HVS at that mode; without HDMI0's regions, an `hdmi=` is asked of the firmware by `SET_TIMING`, after which it keeps the channel on its own two lists, and `hvs` writes its lists over those. Its first frame is the wallpaper, a plane of its own pages under every layer until one covers the screen (see [The display on the Raspberry Pi 5](display.md#the-display-on-the-raspberry-pi-5)). It shows the layers as one display list of planes fetched straight from the clients' pages, each buffer made one run of addresses by the IOMMU in front of the HVS. It writes each list in the half of the list memory the channel isn't fetching, then moves the channel to it. A `damage` cleans the damaged rows' pages out of the caches, and the pointer is a plane on top. A rounded layer's corners are cut by its pixels' alpha, which `hvs` writes 0 there (`corners.l0`). It asks the firmware first whether a display was brought up, since the HVS is otherwise unclocked, and makes sure of its clocks (`fw.l0`). With no region over the HVS, as on QEMU, or no monitor on HDMI0 at boot, it says so and exits 0 |
| `hdmi-audio` | the Raspberry Pi 5's sound driver, which has played a WAV on a board: it serves the audio protocol as `audio` on HDMI0 (see [Sound on the Raspberry Pi 5](sound.md#sound-on-the-raspberry-pi-5)), feeding HDMI0's audio FIFO, the MAI, IEC958 subframes (`iec958.l0`) from a ring of DMA pages through channel 6 of the 40-bit DMA controller, `dma40` (`dma40.l0`), with the MAI, the audio packets and the infoframe set up as Linux's vc4 sets them (`mai.l0`). It waits for `display`, since `hvs` brings HDMI0 up, and plays nothing on a DVI sink. `hdmi-audio probe` reads the channel's and the audio registers, writing none. With no channel, as on QEMU, it says so and exits 0 |
| `virtio-input` | a user-space driver for QEMU's virtio input devices, started once a device. It keeps the event queue's 16 buffers in a DMA page and takes the device's interrupt as a message. `virtio-input keyboard` finds the device whose name holds `Keyboard`, serves `keyboard`, and sends each key event to every port that asked it to `listen`: a `KeyEvent` of the Linux key code, pressed, released or repeated, and the character it types on a US layout, with Shift and Caps Lock, and Ctrl with a letter as its control character. `virtio-input tablet` finds `Tablet`, serves `pointer`, and at each report's end sends a `PointerEvent`: x and y, 0 to 32767, and the buttons; before it, if the scroll wheel turned in the report (`REL_WHEEL`, `REL_HWHEEL`), a `WheelEvent` of its steps, down and right positive. With no such device on the board it says so on stderr and exits 0. Its US layout is `keymap.l0`'s, which `usb-hid` shares |
| `pcie` | the PCIe bus's server, `pcie virt`, for QEMU's virt board (see [USB](devices.md#usb)): reads every function of bus 0 through the ECAM, sizes each memory BAR, puts it in the 32-bit MMIO window, turns on memory space and bus mastering, and serves the pci protocol as `pci`: every function, or those of a class, and an MSI for a driver that asks, set up through the function's MSI or MSI-X capability |
| `sdhci` | a driver for SD cards behind SD Host Controllers, `sdhci pci` those `pcie` finds, QEMU's `sdhci-pci`, and `sdhci pi5` the Raspberry Pi 5's microSD slot, `sdio1`, on interrupt 305 (see [The disk](storage.md#the-disk)): it brings each card up, standard or high-capacity, and serves each as the first `diskN` free, all on one port, telling `fs` of each. Blocks move through the controller's buffer into and out of the client's pages, no DMA; it waits on the controller's interrupt, an MSI or else its INTx line, for what is not done at once, and polls a slot with none. A slot with no card, or no controller on the bus, is said so on stderr, and exits 0 |
| `xhci` | the USB host controller driver and USB's core (see [USB](devices.md#usb)): `xhci pci` drives every xHCI `pci` found, `xhci rp1` the Raspberry Pi 5's two DWC3s on RP1. It halts and resets each controller, gives it its DCBAA, scratchpads, command ring and event ring in DMA pages, and runs it; brings up each device on a root port, one at a time, reading its descriptors and setting its first configuration; and serves the usb protocol as `usb`: devices, their descriptors, claims of interfaces, control transfers, and IN endpoints polled, each report a notice to the client that opened it. It takes each controller's interrupt, an MSI it asks `pcie` for or else its INTx line, as a message, and reads that controller's events; with a controller that has none, as RP1's, or whose line another driver has bound, it polls every event ring, every millisecond when idle. Each controller and each device is a line on stderr. Everything it decides is `core.l0`'s and `bus.l0`'s, pure, tested on the host |
| `usb-hid` | the class driver of USB boot keyboards and mice (see [USB](devices.md#usb)), started once for each: `usb-hid keyboard` claims every boot keyboard, sets the boot protocol and SET_IDLE(0), opens its interrupt endpoint, and serves `keyboard`, sending a `KeyEvent` for each key its reports press or let go, with the character `keymap.l0` says, and repeating a held key itself, 500 ms then every 33 ms; `usb-hid mouse` claims every boot mouse and serves `pointer`, sending a `PointerMotion` of each report. It drives no device, and needs no `dev` line |
| `virtio-blk` | a user-space driver for QEMU's virtio-blk, serving the disk protocol as the first `diskN` free, `disk0` in the demo, and telling `fs` (see [The disk](storage.md#the-disk)). It finds the device, reads its capacity from its config, and sets up its request queue in one DMA page. Each `read` or `write` it checks against the disk's size and the pages the client shared, then has the device move the sectors straight into or out of those pages, a descriptor a page, and waits for the device's interrupt, on a port of its own, for 30 seconds at most, after which it takes the disk to have stopped answering and fails every request. It accepts FLUSH, for `flush`, and RO, refusing a read-only disk's writes itself. With no virtio-blk on the board it says so on stderr and exits 0 |
| `virtio-net` | a user-space driver for QEMU's virtio-net, serving the nic protocol as `nic` (see [The network](network.md#the-network)). It finds the card, takes its address from its config, sets up its receive and transmit queues in DMA pages, and takes its interrupt as a message: each frame the card takes it tells every port that asked it to `listen`, and each frame it is sent it copies into its transmit buffer and waits for the card to take. With no virtio-net on the board it says so on stderr and exits 0 |
| `gem` | the Raspberry Pi 5's network driver, a first draft that has run nowhere, serving the nic protocol as `nic` as `virtio-net` does (see [The network on the Raspberry Pi 5](network.md#the-network-on-the-raspberry-pi-5)). It drives RP1's Cadence GEM and the BCM54213PE PHY behind it over MDIO, takes the board's address from the firmware through the mailbox, and polls its rings and the link, since L0 gives no interrupt for RP1. With no RP1 on the board, as on QEMU, it says so and exits 0 |
| `virtio-rng` | a user-space driver for QEMU's virtio-rng, the entropy device, which draws on the host's `/dev/urandom`, serving the random protocol as `random` (see [Randomness](devices.md#randomness)). It finds the device, sets up its one queue of 8 entries in a DMA page, and for `bytes N` offers the device a buffer to fill, again until it has N, waiting for each as `virtq_wait` does. With no virtio-rng on the board it says so on stderr and exits 0 |
| `inet` | the IPv4 stack, `inet dhcp` or `inet [ADDR/PREFIX [GATEWAY [DNS]]]`: the address, netmask, router and name server a DHCP server gives it, its lease renewed and rebound as the clock's minutes pass, or those given, 10.0.2.15/24 behind 10.0.2.2, with 10.0.2.3 as the name server, by default (see [The network](network.md#the-network)): a client of `nic`, which answers ARP and ICMP echo requests, keeps the neighbours ARP finds, routes to the link or the gateway, gives UDP datagrams to the clients bound to their ports, and sends their datagrams and echo requests, serving the inet protocol as `inet`. With no `nic` within 5 seconds it says there is no card and exits 0 |
| `net` | a client of `inet`: `net` shows the interface, `net stats`, `net arp`, `net dns` and `net tcp` what `inet` counts and knows (the last, its TCP connections and listeners), `net resolve NAME [SERVER [PORT]]` resolves a name, `net ping [-q] HOST [COUNT]` pings, a second apart, and says how long each reply took, failing if none came, `net send HOST PORT TEXT...` sends a datagram, `net recv PORT` waits for one and shows it, and `net echo PORT` listens to a TCP port and sends back what its first connection sends. A HOST is a name, resolved first, or an address |
| `http` | a client of `inet`: `http [-i] [http://]HOST[:PORT][/PATH]` fetches a page over HTTP/1.0 (RFC 1945) through TCP, and writes its body, and with `-i` its header first; says the status, after the page, if it is not 2xx, and fails; HOST is a name, which `inet`'s name server resolves, or an address |
| `tftp` | a client of `inet`: `tftp HOST FILE [PATH]` fetches FILE over TFTP (RFC 1350), from a host named or dotted, in blocks of 512 bytes each acknowledged, asking again for one that does not come within a second, and writes it to PATH or standard output; says what the server says if it refuses |
| `random` | a client of `random`: `random [N]` writes N bytes of the entropy device's, 16 if not given, 1 to 1024, as hex digits and a newline, waiting a second at most for the driver to register; fails, saying why, without one |
| `font` | the font service, serving the font protocol as `font` (`proto/font.l0`) for the faces `code`, `code-bold`, `sans` and `sans-bold`, Source Code Pro and Source Sans 3 in `/etc/fonts`. As it starts it keeps the strikes `tools/mkstrike` made at the build, `/etc/fonts/FACE-H.strike`, Source Code Pro's regular and bold at 16, 32 and 64, each in pages of its own. `strike FACE H` gives that face at line height H, 8 to 128: one kept, or one it makes then (`glyphs.l0`), reading the face's TrueType file the first time one of it is made, kept in a `Map` by face and height, never freed; it shares those pages with each asker, read only (`shm_share_read`), once each, and answers where the asker sees them, `VA N`. `faces` names the faces; `stats` says how many strikes it has made, and each one kept, its size and how many processes it was given to |
| `fonts` | a client of `font`: `fonts` lists its faces, how many strikes it has built and each one it keeps; `fonts strike FACE H` asks for one, as a program that draws text does, and says how many pages it came in |
| `disk` | a client of the disks: `disk list` lists them, each name and size; of `disk0`, or of the disk named first, as `disk disk1 info`: `disk info` prints its size; `disk read SECTOR [COUNT]` prints COUNT sectors, 1 unless given, as `hexdump -C` does, a `*` for repeated lines; `disk write SECTOR WORDS...` writes the words, spaced, and a newline as sector SECTOR, zeros after them; `disk zero SECTOR [COUNT]` writes COUNT sectors of zeros, 1 unless given, in one request. A refusal goes to stderr, with status 1 |
| `fs` | the file server (see [Volumes](storage.md#volumes)): a client of every disk, `disk0` and the rest, which mounts each volume on them, of L0S's file system or FAT32 (see [FAT32 volumes](fat32.md#fat32-volumes)), at `/Volumes/LABEL`, with its partition's UUID, and serves the fs protocol for them, the kernel's requests for every program's file calls there, in directories at any depth, and `list`, `mkdir`, `stat`, `chmod`, `df`, `format`, `relabel`, `unmount`, `rescan`, `partition`, `erase`, `attach` and `mkfs`; then registers as `fs`, though no disk has a volume, or a partition table, yet. It reads each disk's GPT, or with none its MBR, and makes partitions in a GPT, each with its UUID, and volumes on them, L0FS or FAT32, and volumes on an MBR's partitions too, whose type byte alone it changes, every disk's only writer. A disk registered after it started is taken on when its driver asks it to `attach` it. It keeps each client's file window as the kernel's notice tells of it, reads and writes a client's data there for `readw` and `writew`, up to 64 KB a request, writes a file's data alone and its size and chain at its close or its client's exit (see [Limits](storage.md#limits)), watches the client, and gives the window back with `shm_unmap`, with its handles, once the client exits (see [File windows](kernel.md#a-processs-memory)). With no `disk0` within 5 seconds it says so on stderr and exits 0. `fs ram [MB]` serves no disk but one volume in memory instead (see [Booting from a volume](boot.md#booting-from-a-volume)) |
| `wm` | the compositor (see [The desktop](desktop.md#the-desktop)): waits for `display`, `keyboard` and `pointer`, and a second for `clock`, paints the wallpaper on the desktop (`/etc/wallpaper.qoi`, `/etc/wallpaper2x.qoi` at scale 2, the image the display driver showed first), sets the pointer's arrow, draws the top bar, reads every app's manifest and paints the launcher's page of them, and serves `compositor`. Its top bar names the front window's app; its launcher starts apps. At scale 2 it lays the desktop out at half the screen's size each way and draws it at the screen's resolution. It needs no `dev` line |
| `windows` | a client of `compositor`: prints its `windows` reply, the windows front first and where the pointer is |
| `draw` | a client of `compositor`: sends it `desktop` and the command its arguments make (`line`, `circle`, `disc`, `rect`, `fill`, `clear`, `char`, the toolkit's `round`, `button` and `icon X Y NAME [SCALE [COLOR]]`, `splash`, `restore`, `size`, `font`), which `wm` draws on the desktop. Colors may be Adwaita's by name (`blue_3`, `dark_4`, `accent`…). It checks the command with the toolkit's parser first, so a bad one fails without asking `wm`. It prints the size, or the error on stderr with status 1. With no arguments it lists the commands |
| `widgets` | Widgets, the toolkit's gallery ([user/lib/ui](toolkit.md)): a window with a toolbar (Refresh, and End Process, sensitive only with a row selected) over a data grid of sample processes, sortable by any column, selected by click or the arrow keys, and along its foot a tab bar of three tabs, selected by a click, each closed by its close button but the last. It's the scaffolding for a process monitor; it reads no real processes and ends none |
| `about` | About L0S, which the L0S menu opens: a window of the machine's CPU (`machine`), the memory the kernel has given out (`mem_info`), how many processes live, and the uptime (`clock_ns`), under the name and the logo's icon |
| `disks` | Disks, `run disks &`, as GNOME Disks is: a sidebar of drives, every disk registered, each its size and name, one shown at a time, chosen by click; the drive's name (`disk1`), size and partitioning; a bar of its partitions and the free space between them, each as wide as its share of the disk, one selected by click or Left and Right; and the selected part's size, usage, files, contents, partition type, partition UUID and sectors. It reads the GPT and each partition's first sector through `disk` itself, so a partition no server serves still shows, and asks `fs` how full each mounted volume is, through one reply port. Refresh has `fs` `rescan`, takes on any disk registered since, and reads the drive again. Format Disk..., beside Refresh, asks in a destructive dialog, then has `fs` `erase` the drive, a new, empty GPT in place of every partition, a GPT's or an MBR's, its volumes unmounted first. Under the bar, one button for the selected part: on free space, Create Partition... asks its size in kB, all the free space unless another is typed, with the space it leaves said as it is typed, then has `fs` `partition` it there and selects the new partition; on a mounted volume, Format Volume... asks in a dialog, then has `fs` `format` it; on any other partition, Format... asks the new volume's type, L0FS or FAT32, by a toggle, and its name, a label `fs` will take (FAT32's of up to 11), and has `fs` `mkfs` it by the partition's UUID, mounted at `/Volumes/NAME`. A disk with an MBR, not a GPT, shows its partitions and their volumes, its partitioning Master Boot Record, and offers Format Volume... and Format... as on a GPT, so that Raspberry Pi OS's ext4 rootfs can be made L0FS or FAT32, but no Create Partition..., L0S making no partitions in an MBR. Each dialog's entry has the focus as it opens, and Enter is its button; a refusal is said in the line at the foot. With no disk it shows a status page |
| `files` | Files, `run files &`, as GNOME 45's Files is, in a window that draws its own header bars (`csd`): a sidebar of places under a header bar titled Files, Computer (`/`), Volumes (`/Volumes`), and each volume with how much of it is free; and the place shown, under a header bar of Back and Forward, a path bar of where it is, whose parts go there, a toggle between a grid of icons and a list (name, size, kind, sortable), the menu (New Folder..., Refresh, the other view) and the window's close button; and a line of what was last done. The libraries are in `common/`, shared by the kernel, every program and
the host tools, and in `user/lib/`, grouped by what they are: `sys/`, the
kernel's calls and what programs do alike; `proto/`, the protocols of
the servers, as their clients speak them; `format/`, data as bytes,
pure, which the host tools link too; `gfx/`, drawing; and `ui/`, the
toolkit. Each is linked into every program (`ULIB`), or into those that
need it, by the Makefile's `LIBS_` lists. L0 has one namespace, so a
name in a library is taken in every program that links it.
